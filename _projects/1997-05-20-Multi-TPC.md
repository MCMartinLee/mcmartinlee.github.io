# A Multimodal Dataset for Three-Party Conversations with Speech, Motion, and Gaze

🔗 GitHub: [https://github.com/MCMartinLee/Multi-TPC](https://github.com/MCMartinLee/Multi-TPC)

---

## Why I Worked on This

A lot of conversational AI research focuses on **two-person conversations**.

But if you think about it, most real conversations aren’t like that.

They look more like:
- three people talking  
- people interrupting each other  
- someone reacting while someone else is speaking  
- attention constantly shifting  

That complexity is exactly what makes conversation hard—and interesting.

So the question became:

> How do we study conversation when it’s actually messy and multi-party?

---

## The Problem

Most existing datasets:
- focus on **single speaker** or **dyadic interaction**  
- don’t capture **who is looking at whom**  
- don’t model **listener behavior properly**  

But in a three-person conversation:
- you’re not just speaking or listening  
- you’re constantly deciding *who* you’re engaging with  

That’s something we were missing.

---

## What Multi-TPC Is

Multi-TPC is a dataset designed to capture **real, spontaneous three-person conversations**, with multiple modalities recorded at the same time.

We capture:

- 🎙 **Speech** (audio + transcripts)  
- 🕺 **Full-body motion** (MoCap)  
- 👀 **Gaze** (who looks at whom)  

And most importantly, everything is **time-aligned**.

---

## Why Three People Changes Everything

In dyadic conversation:
> A talks → B listens  

In triadic conversation:
> A talks → B and C both react (differently)

Now you have:
- competing attention  
- shifting engagement  
- more complex turn-taking  

This creates a much richer interaction space:
- Who will speak next?  
- Who is the listener actually responding to?  
- How does gaze influence turn-taking?  

---

## What Makes This Dataset Different

A few things we really focused on:

### 1. Capturing Both Speaker *and* Listener Behavior
Not just what people say—but how they react.

### 2. Full-Body Motion (Not Just Head or Hands)
Subtle movements matter for communication.

### 3. Gaze as a First-Class Signal
Who you look at often matters more than what you say.

### 4. Precise Synchronization
All modalities are aligned so you can study timing accurately.

---

## How We Collected It (Short Version)

We built a pipeline that captures everything together:

- Motion capture using **Vicon**  
- Gaze + audio using **D-Lab setup**  
- Additional processing for:
  - prosody (pitch, intensity)  
  - transcripts  
  - alignment  

All signals are synchronized so you can analyze interactions frame-by-frame.

---

## What You Can Do With It

This dataset enables research on:

- 🗣️ Turn-taking prediction  
- 👁️ Gaze and attention modeling  
- 🤖 Conversational agents  
- 🎭 Gesture and motion generation  
- 🧠 Social interaction analysis  

---

## How This Connects to My Work

This dataset is actually the foundation for a lot of what I do.

It supports questions like:
- when should an agent speak?  
- where should it look?  
- how should it move while listening?  

And it directly feeds into systems like RESPOND, where timing and interaction really matter.

---

## Final Thought

If we want AI to participate in conversation naturally,  
we can’t just model speech.

We need to model:
- attention  
- motion  
- interaction dynamics  

Multi-TPC is a step toward that.
